# AE26 Module 2 - Listening Actively and Reading Critically

## Reading Guide / 阅读说明

Module 1 把你放进了大学的 [[Academic Conversation]]；Module 2 开始教你如何先把别人的 contribution 听懂、读懂并留下可用的痕迹。这不是“记得越多越好”，而是让 listening、reading 和 note-taking 都有目的地服务后续思考、写作与 assessment。

> [!summary] 一句话主线
> **Active learners do not merely record information; they select, reorganize, question, and connect it for a future purpose.**
>
> 主动学习者不是把信息原样搬走，而是根据未来用途去选择、重组、追问和连接它。

## 1. Module Map / 模块脉络

~~~text
enter the academic conversation
-> listen for ideas rather than transcribing words
-> choose a note structure that matches the material
-> recognize the genre and anatomy of a scholarly text
-> read sections in an order that matches your purpose
-> preview and question before close reading
-> annotate for gist while reading
-> reflect and adapt the strategy for university work
~~~

## 2. Active Listening Becomes Usable Notes / 主动听讲如何变成可用笔记

课程首先打破一个常见误解：note-taking 不等于 transcription。如果只追求把每句话打下来，你的 attention 会被 wording 占满，反而来不及判断哪些是 concept、example、relationship 或 question。

> **Good notes are a selective reconstruction of meaning, not a verbatim copy of speech.**

因此，听课时要优先抓：

- key concepts and discipline-specific terms；
- main claims and the reasons supporting them；
- examples, analogies, equations, diagrams, and contrasts；
- 教授特别重复、停顿或强调的内容；
- 自己没有听懂、需要回看或追问的地方。

### Before, During, and After Class

| phase | useful move | why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| before | preview slides, headings, readings, and unfamiliar terms | 先建立骨架，课上更容易看见新信息放在哪里 |
| during | paraphrase, abbreviate, mark relationships and questions | 把听到的话转换成自己可以处理的结构 |
| after | review, fill gaps, revise, and connect | 趁记忆还在，将临时记录变成可复习的知识 |

这个循环连向 [[Active Note-Taking]]：记笔记是一个 processing loop，不是一次性的 capture event。

### Handwriting or Laptop?

课程不给绝对答案，而是要你根据 [[Scholarly Situation]] 选工具：

- Laptop 速度快、容易搜索和整理，但容易诱发 verbatim transcription 和 distraction。
- Handwriting 速度较慢，迫使你压缩与改写，也更适合 equations、diagrams 和 spatial layouts。
- 真正的标准是：这个工具是否让你保持 attention，并产生之后能用的 notes。

### Four Note Structures

| method | structure | best fit |
|---|---|---|
| Cornell | main notes + cue/questions column + summary | 需要课后自测和快速复习 |
| Outline | hierarchy of headings and indented details | 讲授本身有清楚层级和顺序 |
| Mapping | nodes, arrows, and spatial relationships | 需要看见概念之间的网络 |
| Charting | columns or categories compared across rows | 需要比较 theories, cases, causes, or properties |

方法不是身份标签。同一人可以在 history lecture 用 Outline，在 chemistry comparison 用 Charting，再在期末复习时把内容重组成 Mapping。

> [!important] Recording boundary / 录制边界
> Lecture materials, recordings, slides, and classroom contributions may be protected by privacy, copyright, and course rules. **Do not record, copy, or redistribute them without explicit instructor permission.**

## 3. Academic Conventions Are Reading Signals / 学术惯例也是阅读导航

Academic conventions 包括表层的 citation、terminology 和 section headings，也包括更深的 conceptual moves：作者如何从 **they say** 走向 **I say**，如何说明自己与已有研究的关系。

### Shared Expectations Across Disciplines

- precise and accurate wording；
- discipline-appropriate terminology；
- qualified claims rather than sweeping generalizations；
- inclusive language that avoids bias and stereotypes；
- citations that give credit, expose the evidence trail, and make the writer accountable。

Citation styles 可以是 APA、MLA、Chicago 或 IEEE，但它们的核心功能相同：将这一次 contribution 接回现有的 [[Academic Conversation]]。

## 4. Anatomy of an Academic Research Article / 研究论文的解剖图

多数 [[Academic Research Article]] 由 academic researchers 写作，通常有数千字，报告作者设计并完成的 investigation，同时把结果与其他 scholars 的工作连起来。

### Common Front and Back Matter

| section | what it tells you | how a reader can use it |
|---|---|---|
| title | 研究对象或问题 | 快速判断 topic 和 article type |
| keywords | 学科内的标准搜索词 | 继续检索相关研究 |
| abstract | purpose, major result, implications 的压缩 | 判断 relevance；不应只引用 abstract 代替原文 |
| introduction / literature review | background, gap, motivation, prior scholarship | 理解问题为什么值得研究，并追到原始 sources |
| references | 文中使用的完整来源 | 扩展 source network，找到对话中的其他参与者 |

### Humanities and Sciences Do Not Use the Same Middle

~~~text
Humanities tendency:
Title -> Abstract -> Introduction/Literature Review -> Argument-based body -> Conclusion -> References

Sciences tendency:
Title -> Abstract -> Introduction/Literature Review -> Methods -> Results -> Discussion -> Conclusion -> References
~~~

- **Methods** 说明研究怎样完成，让读者判断过程是否合理、可验证或可复现。
- **Results** 呈现数据和 observed outcomes。
- **Discussion** 解释 results 意味着什么，如何连回旧研究，以及有哪些 limitations。
- Humanities articles 往往不使用标准 IMRaD 结构，而是用 primary texts 和分析逐步支撑 argument。

## 5. Read in the Order Your Purpose Requires / 阅读不必永远从第一行到最后一行

学术阅读的基本单位不是 page count，而是 purpose。

### First-pass relevance check

~~~text
title and keywords
-> abstract
-> discussion and conclusion
-> decide whether the article fits your question
-> introduction/literature review for context
-> methods and results for close evaluation
-> references for the next sources
~~~

这不是偷懒，而是根据 section function 分配 attention。如果目标是找 background，先读 literature review；如果要判断某个 claim 是否站得住，就必须进入 methods、results 和 limitations。

课程对 Carl Zimmer 文章的使用还提醒了一个重要边界：

> **A scientific paper is a current, reviewable status report, not a revelation of absolute truth.**

科学论文使用 jargon 和特定 genre rules，并通过 peer review 让外部专家检查弱点。但 peer review 不是把文章变成永恒真理；它只是在当前证据下提高 reliability。尚未经 peer review 的 preprint 尤其需要保持 skepticism。

对一篇 science paper 做快速质量检查时，至少要问：

- Is the study based on a few cases or a sufficiently large sample?
- Does it distinguish correlation from causation?
- Do the authors present evidence that actually supports the conclusion?

## 6. Textbooks: Read for Application / 教材不是报纸

Textbooks 由 scholars 写作，但通常不像研究论文那样直接提交一个新 contribution。阅读它们仍然需要 [[Critical Reading]]，而且首先要问：**Why am I reading this, and how will I use it?**

| assessment purpose | information to extract |
|---|---|
| multiple-choice test | definitions, distinctions, details, and rapid-recall questions |
| essay | main ideas, relationships, and explanations in your own words |
| lab/data evaluation | methods, variables, data patterns, and interpretation |
| presentation/report | research gist, evidence, and communicable structure |

反复扫过同一段文字只会带来 familiarity，不保证 retention 或 application。更好的做法是像和一个会挑战你的朋友对话：听懂对方，用自己的话回应，并产生一个 output。

### Two small habits

- **Input-based habit:** read for a fixed period, such as 15 minutes daily.
- **Output-based habit:** produce something concrete, such as a short summary or half a page of notes.

前者降低开始成本，后者防止阅读只停留在 exposure。

## 7. Critical Reading: From Student to Student-Scholar

[[Critical Reading]] 是一个 active, directed process。它不仅问“这篇文章说了什么”，还问“它怎样说服读者，这样写带来了什么效果”。

| passive reading | critical reading |
|---|---|
| every text is read the same way | speed and pattern change with genre and purpose |
| begin without questions | form questions before reading |
| skip unfamiliar terms and ideas | mark confusion and investigate it |
| stop when the eyes reach the end | pause, reflect, and revise notes |

### Questions Before Reading

Preview headings and visuals, then ask from broad to specific:

1. What topic or issue is the writer covering?
2. What is the thesis or main argument?
3. What reasons and examples support it?
4. How are those supports organized?
5. Which details matter to the writer's purpose?
6. How does diction or word choice help produce the intended effect?

同时带上你自己的 course or project question。如果你的 research topic 只关心一本书中的某一方面，就不需要对每一页分配相同深度的 attention。

## 8. Annotation and Notes for Gist / 标注的核心是思考，不是颜色

Annotation 是 critical reading 留在文本上的痕迹：

- identify key concepts and claims；
- rephrase them in your own words；
- organize relationships；
- ask questions and mark confusion；
- connect the text to other ideas or sources；
- record agreement, doubt, implication, or a possible use。

只是把“有趣的句子”大量涂黄，并没有完成上述 cognitive work。

### Notes for Gist

> **A gist note is a brief, provisional statement of what matters in a paragraph for the reader's later purpose.**

课程的实作原则是：

1. keep highlighting minimal;
2. aim for roughly one gist note per paragraph;
3. avoid full-sentence copying;
4. use point form and your own words;
5. record not only the author's point but, where useful, your response or question.

~~~text
Before reading: preview headings and visuals
-> While reading: mark claims, questions, details, and connections
-> After reading: state the main point and why it matters
~~~

这个三阶段流程将 annotation 从“在 PDF 上留颜色”变成“为之后的 summary、analysis 和 writing 准备中间材料”。

## 9. Activities and Assessment / 活动真正训练什么

### Note-taking Practice

从 Module 1 的 scholar videos 中选一个，尝试一种新的 note-taking method，并思考从 high school 到 university 时方法需要怎样调整。

### Academic Conventions Quiz

5 道题、unlimited attempts，用来检查对 academic conventions 和 article structure 的识别。本笔记不保存个人答案或尝试记录。

### Annotation Practice

用 Zimmer 文章的连续段落练习 gist notes，按 preview -> annotate -> identify main ideas 三个阶段完成。

### Final Check-in: Adapting Strategies

用 `200-400 words` 反思高中时的 active listening / critical reading 方法哪些有效、哪些失效，以及你准备如何改造它们。Rubric 看重 module concepts、genuine reflection 和 relevant detail。这里保留学习目标，不生成可直接提交的个人回答。

## 10. Key Phrases / 一句话对应关系

- **Active listening** -> listening to identify and process meaning, not merely hear or record words.
- **Good notes** -> a selective reconstruction of ideas for later use.
- **Note-taking method** -> a structure chosen to match the material and the future task.
- **Academic convention** -> a recurring surface practice or conceptual move that helps a scholarly community communicate.
- **Academic research article** -> a structured contribution that reports an investigation and connects it to prior scholarship.
- **Abstract** -> a compressed relevance check, not a substitute for reading and citing the article itself.
- **Methods** -> how the investigation was carried out.
- **Results** -> what data or outcomes the investigation produced.
- **Discussion** -> what the results mean, how they fit previous work, and where the study is limited.
- **Peer review** -> evaluation by outside specialists before publication; quality control, not a guarantee of absolute truth.
- **Strategic reading** -> changing reading order and depth according to purpose.
- **Critical reading** -> analyzing both what a text says and how its choices shape the reader's response.
- **Annotation** -> visible evidence of questioning, paraphrasing, organizing, connecting, and evaluating while reading.
- **Notes for gist** -> brief, own-word statements of what matters in each section for a later writing purpose.
- **Passive rereading** -> repeated exposure that can increase familiarity without producing usable understanding.
